Board Position Descriptions

The scope of each board position, stated for adoption as board policy and for signed acceptance by each holder upon election. One description per position; a signed acceptance is filed with the corporate records.

How these descriptions work

Each position's duties bind its holder through the bylaws and the Washington Nonprofit Corporation Act from the moment of election; that is the source of legal force. What this instrument adds is definition and record: the board adopts these descriptions by resolution as the settled scope of each position, and each person elected signs an acceptance acknowledging the office, its duties, and the governing documents. The signed acceptance is evidence of notice and agreement, kept with the corporate records by the Secretary.

What every position shares

  • The statutory standard of conduct: good faith, the care of an ordinarily prudent person in a like position, and the corporation's best interests (RCW 24.03A.495 for directors, RCW 24.03A.590 for officers).
  • The Conflict of Interest Policy, with prompt disclosure, recusal, and an annual signed disclosure statement.
  • No compensation for board service; reasonable expense reimbursement under board policy.
  • Annual election; service until a successor is elected and qualified; resignation by written notice.
  • Confidential handling of personnel, participant, donor, and deliberative records, during and after service.

Adoption

Adopted as board policy by resolution of the Board of Directors on , recorded in the minutes. Amendment follows the same path as any board policy. These descriptions restate and organize duties from the bylaws; where they differ from the bylaws, the bylaws govern.

Companion to Bylaws Articles IV, V, and VI. Owner: Governance. Filed with the corporate records.

Chair of the Board

Authority: Bylaws Sections 6.1, 6.5 · Resolution 2 of the organizational meeting · RCW 24.03A.585, .590

Purpose

The Chair leads the board's work: convening it, focusing it, and connecting it to the corporation's leadership. The Chair holds the office of president under the title Chair of the Board and performs its statutory functions, so the corporation always has a presiding executive function on the face of its record.

Responsibilities

  • Preside at meetings of the board; set agendas with the Secretary and officers.
  • Serve as liaison between the board and executive leadership.
  • Hold the office of president under the title Chair of the Board and perform its statutory functions, per Resolution 2.
  • Sign adopted instruments where the bylaws or a resolution call for the Chair's signature.
  • Verify the quarterly records deposit under the Document Retention and Destruction Policy.

Limits

The Chair must be a director, exercises no unilateral authority over the corporation except as the board authorizes, may not simultaneously serve as Secretary, and votes as one director among equals.

Acceptance. I accept the office of Chair of the Board of Luminara Institute of Light, acknowledge receipt of the bylaws and the Conflict of Interest Policy, and agree to perform the duties of the office as described here and in the bylaws, subject to the standards of conduct of RCW 24.03A.

Vice Chair

Authority: Bylaws Sections 6.1, 6.6 · RCW 24.03A.590

Purpose

The Vice Chair is the board's continuity: ready to carry the Chair's duties the moment the Chair is absent or unable, so the board never loses its presiding function.

Responsibilities

  • Perform the duties of the Chair whenever the Chair is absent or unable to act.
  • Carry standing or special duties assigned by the Chair or the board.
  • Maintain working familiarity with the corporation's governing documents, finances, and active matters sufficient to preside without notice.

Limits

Acting authority runs only while the Chair is absent or unable, and carries the same boundaries as the Chair's own: no unilateral authority over the corporation, and one vote among equals.

Secretary

Authority: Bylaws Sections 5.11, 6.1, 6.9 · Article X · RCW 24.03A.585, .590

Purpose

The Secretary is the keeper of the corporation's memory: its minutes, notices, records, and governing documents. When anyone asks what the corporation decided and when, the Secretary's records are the answer.

Responsibilities

  • Ensure accurate minutes are kept for board meetings and for committees exercising board authority, recording attendance, actions, votes, recusals, and conflicts disclosed.
  • Give and record the notices the bylaws require for meetings and actions.
  • Maintain the corporate records: articles, bylaws, minutes, resolutions, policies, filings, and adopted instruments, in the "Corporate Records" drive and the hardcopy set.
  • Administer the quarterly records deposit; receive signed disclosures, acceptances, and certifications for filing.
  • Certify adopted documents and record adoption dates upon them.

Limits

The Secretary may delegate ministerial recordkeeping while retaining oversight responsibility, and may not simultaneously serve as Chair or President.

Acceptance. I accept the office of Secretary of Luminara Institute of Light, acknowledge receipt of the bylaws and the Conflict of Interest Policy, and agree to perform the duties of the office as described here and in the bylaws, subject to the standards of conduct of RCW 24.03A.

Treasurer

Authority: Bylaws Sections 6.1, 6.10 · Article X · RCW 24.03A.585, .590

Purpose

The Treasurer is the board's eyes on the money: building the budget, watching the controls, and telling the board the financial truth on a regular rhythm, so no financial surprise reaches the corporation unexamined.

Responsibilities

  • Prepare the annual budget or financial plan for board approval; report regularly on financial condition, variances, restricted funds, and material developments.
  • Oversee internal controls, banking relationships, deposits, and disbursement authorizations under board policy.
  • Oversee tax filings, charitable registrations, and financial compliance calendars.
  • Steward donor-restricted funds in accordance with their restrictions.
  • Support the board's audit, review, or financial-oversight determinations.

Limits

No borrowing or indebtedness without board resolution; no loans to directors or officers ever; disbursements follow the authorizations the board sets; the board may require bonding.

Director

Authority: Bylaws Article IV, Sections 4.12, 4.13 · RCW 24.03A.495

Purpose

Every director, officer or not, carries the same fiduciary charge: the corporation's affairs are managed under the board's authority, and each seat at the table is a share of that responsibility.

Responsibilities

  • Prepare for and attend board meetings; review financial and program information before voting.
  • Act in good faith, with prudent care, in the corporation's best interests.
  • Disclose conflicts of interest promptly and recuse from interested matters.
  • Support prudent stewardship of the corporation's resources and its compliance obligations.
  • Carry any portfolio or committee assignment the board records, for the period recorded.

Limits

No director acts alone for the corporation: authority belongs to the board acting as a body, or to those the board authorizes. Directors serve staggered three-year terms, at most two consecutive full terms before a required year away, and receive no pay for board service.
