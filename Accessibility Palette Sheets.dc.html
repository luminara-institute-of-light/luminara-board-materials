Sheet 1 · Low vision and contrast sensitivity

Condition responded to: reduced visual acuity and contrast sensitivity, including age-related change, cataract, and partial sight. What helps is not different hues but stronger separation between figure and ground. WCAG AA requires 4.5:1 for body-size text; seven of the ten base colors already pass on the ivory ground and three receive a deepened twin for text duty. The base color remains the brand color everywhere else.

Sheet 2 · Red-green color vision deficiency

Condition responded to: deuteranopia and deuteranomaly (green cone absent or shifted) and protanopia and protanomaly (red cone absent or shifted), together the most common forms: roughly eight percent of men and half a percent of women. Red-green hue distinctions collapse; what survives is luminance, the lightness channel. A pair is safe when its lightness separation is large; it is risky when two hues differ mainly in redness or greenness at similar lightness.

The response, and what it deliberately is not. Deepening Jewel Emerald was tested and makes the magenta pair worse, not better: magenta sits below emerald in lightness, so separation must come from lifting the green, and Drew's magenta is never altered. The response is a chart accent: in data graphics, org charts, and any context where Internal Communications and Branding & Outreach (or the two deep greens) appear side by side, Branding & Outreach plots as #45A873 (Emerald Chart Accent). Pair contrast against magenta rises from 1.37:1 to 2.11:1 and against Growth Green from 1.06:1 to 1.64:1. Brand surfaces keep Jewel Emerald #2E8556 unchanged; the accent exists only where colors must be told apart at a glance. Where even that is not enough, the governing rule on the last sheet applies.

Sheet 3 · Blue-yellow color vision deficiency

Condition responded to: tritanopia and tritanomaly (blue cone absent or shifted), the rare form: on the order of one in ten thousand, affecting men and women equally. Blue-yellow distinctions collapse; blues, teals, and violets draw together, as do yellows and warm midtones. The computed pair contrasts on the institute's blue axis:

The response. The cool cluster (Interface Blue, Inquiry Teal, Meaning Violet, Radiant Magenta) sits within seven percent of one lightness value, so no hue adjustment can separate it for tritan vision without leaving the brand; the separation must come from lightness steps and labels. Where two or more of the cluster are co-plotted, plot them at different lightness levels: take the darker member from this sheet's base value and the lighter member from the dark-ground column on Sheet 1 (a computed lift of eighteen percent). With one exception the cluster rarely shares a chart; the labels rule carries the rest.

Sheet 4 · Complete color blindness

Condition responded to: achromatopsia, the absence of color perception: roughly one in thirty thousand people, who see the palette as pure luminance. This sheet shows the gray each color actually presents. Where two grays match, those colors are indistinguishable, and only position, label, shape, or pattern separates them.

The response. Five of the ten colors land in one merged gray band (#585858 to #616161): to an achromatopic reader, Research, Technology, Lumina Lingua, Finance, and Internal Communications are the same color. No palette that keeps these brand hues can fix that in gray; the fix is structural and already adopted as the governing rule: every color-coded element carries its label, and printed materials stay legible photocopied in black and white, which is also the test any staff member can run without tools.

Sheet 5 · Photosensitivity and visual stress

Condition responded to: light sensitivity and pattern-related visual stress, including migraine and post-concussion sensitivity. What helps is a softened ground, reduced saturation over large areas, and no high-frequency contrast patterns. The document ivory #FBF7EA already sits below pure white; for large fields, the palette softens to these tints, blended three quarters of the way toward ivory. Tints are grounds only; they never carry meaning alone and never carry text without an ink-dark foreground.

The governing rule: never color alone

Any meaning a color carries is also carried by a word, shape, or pattern. This rule governs every sheet above. Accessibility in this system is achieved first by labeling and iconography, and only second by the luminance engineering on these sheets; no chart, map, badge, or document in the institute's materials may rely on hue as the sole carrier of information. This is the accessibility floor beneath the palette, adopted alongside the every-page identity rule.

Prevalence figures follow published vision-science reporting; pair values and gray equivalents are computed from the hex values by WCAG 2.1 relative luminance. The widely used Okabe-Ito colorblind-safe palette served as the external reference standard when testing the chart accent. Adoption path: these sheets go to Drew Stone for color review; adopted values then join the Branding Style Sheet as application rows. Until then, every value on this page is a computed proposal.
