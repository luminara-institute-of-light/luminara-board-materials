Document Execution and Signing Protocol

Expectations and rules for signing the corporation's governing documents, in person and electronically.

Scope

This protocol applies to every document requiring a director's or officer's signature: bylaws, board resolutions and consents, minutes, policies, disclosure statements, agreements, and filings. It establishes two accepted methods of execution and one standard of record.

Accepted methods

MethodRequirements
Wet signatureA full-color printout of the final document, signed in ink in blue or black. Signers date every signature. No page is separated from the set.
Electronic signatureApplied to the final document held in the corporation's shared drive, by the signer's own account, with the platform's audit record retained. Electronic signatures carry the same legal effect as wet signatures under Washington's Uniform Electronic Transactions Act, chapter 1.80 RCW, and the federal Electronic Signatures in Global and National Commerce Act, and satisfy the written-consent provisions of Article V of the bylaws.

A document may be executed by a combination of both methods. Signatures on separate counterparts together constitute one instrument.

Standard of record

VersionOnly the final version circulated by the Secretary may be signed. A marked, edited, or reprinted copy is not executable.
ScansWet-signed documents are returned as a single color PDF, at least 300 dpi, every page present, uncropped and unrotated. Photographs of pages are not accepted. Faxed copies are accepted only where a scan is unavailable, and are replaced by a scan when one becomes possible.
NamingDate, document, signer: 2026-09-01 Bylaws Furness signed.pdf
ReturnSigned documents reach the Secretary within five business days of circulation.
DeliverySigned documents are returned to the Secretary at the Secretary’s @luminara.institute address or uploaded directly to the Corporate Records drive. Executed documents are not exchanged through personal email accounts or messaging applications.
OriginalsInk originals are delivered to the Secretary and enter the hardcopy record set held at the principal office. Signers do not retain the only original of any corporate document.

Authority to sign

The Chair signs on the corporation's behalf as authorized by the board. The Secretary attests governing documents and certifies copies. The Treasurer signs financial instruments within the limits set by board resolution. No other person signs for the corporation without express board authorization.

A signature is personal to the signer. No person may sign in another’s name, apply another’s electronic signature, or execute a document from an account that is not their own. Shared credentials are not permitted for execution. A signer who wishes to authorize execution on their behalf must do so by a written authorization filed with the Secretary and approved by the board.

Unavailability

If a signer is unreachable or unable to sign within the return period, the Secretary records the absence in the signature log and notifies the Chair. The instrument may be completed if it still carries every signature the bylaws or applicable law require; if it does not, execution is held until the signature is obtained or the board acts to replace the requirement.

Custody and verification

The Secretary maintains the executed set in the corporation's shared drive designated "Corporate Records," logs each signature received, and files the platform's audit record alongside each electronically signed document. Executed documents enter the quarterly hardcopy deposit, which the Chair verifies as matching the digital record, under the Document Retention and Destruction Policy.

Corrections

An error in an executed document is corrected by a new signed instrument, never by alteration of a signed page. Superseded versions are retained and marked as superseded.
